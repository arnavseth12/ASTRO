THE PROJECT

An independent project in progress.

ASTRO is being developed as a public workspace for astronomy and astrophysics: sourced imagery, scientific catalogues, physics tools, and research pathways in one place.

WHY ASTRO EXISTS

Space deserves more than a feed of pictures.

The aim is to bring observations, scientific context, physics tools, and research pathways together in one place. A visitor should be able to see an object, ask a question, explore the data, and understand what the evidence does — and does not — say.

ASTRO is being built progressively. Features marked as planned or in development are not presented as finished products. The project will favour traceable sources, explicit units, documented assumptions, and honest uncertainty over impressive-looking but unsupported claims.

SOURCE DIRECTORYDATA AND ATTRIBUTION

Follow the original record.

01·NASA ScienceMission science, imagery, and research explainers ↗02·NASA Image and Video LibraryPublic NASA media records ↗03·NASA Exoplanet ArchivePlanetary systems, candidates, and parameters ↗04·MAST / STScIMission observations and data products ↗05·JPL Solar System DynamicsEphemerides and small-body data ↗06·Launch Library 2Launch and mission event records ↗

Data is attributed to the provider and source record where practical. NASA media is not automatically public domain in every case; credit lines and asset-specific rights must be checked before reuse. Scientific values should retain units, uncertainty, retrieval dates, and model assumptions where available.
